# Motto

Fixed text of session 4 of 4 of the identity. Show `Briefing` word for word; ask the `Dig` questions as written; show the `Sharpness tests` as a checklist; say the `Link` as written.

## Briefing

**Strategist:** Session 4 of 4: the **Motto**.

**Where it comes from.** Agency practice, no single author.

**The idea.** The motto is the few words the brand signs its copy with. It is copy, not a part of the logo: the logo stands alone, and the motto is never set beside it or under it. It does not describe the product and it does not repeat the promise: it is the attitude, said so that a person can say it back after one reading.

**Why now.** It comes last because it needs everything before it: the promise to stand on, the voice to speak in, the type to be set in.

**What we will leave with.** The motto in the language of your market, the places where it is read, and the finalists it beat.

From here the Copywriter leads.

## Dig

1. Where will the motto be read most: at the end of a text, in a bio, at the end of a video, on a package, in a signature?
2. Who speaks in it: the brand about itself, the brand to the customer, or the customer?
3. Is there a phrase you or your customers already say about the brand, one that keeps coming back?

## Sharpness tests

- A person can say it back after reading it once.
- A competitor from Positioning could not sign it.
- It says the attitude; it does not say the Main promise over again.
- It breaks no line of "We never write".
- It holds alone, as one line of text, at the size of an email signature.

## Link

Next: the brand on real things. The Art Director puts the logo, the colors and the type on the surfaces your brand will live on.
