# Typography

Fixed text of session 3 of 4 of the identity. Show `Briefing` word for word; ask the `Dig` questions as written; show the `Sharpness tests` as a checklist; say the `Link` as written.

## Briefing

**Strategist:** Session 3 of 4: **Typography**.

**Where it comes from.** Robert Bringhurst, "The Elements of Typographic Style", 1992: "Typography exists to honor content."

**The idea.** Most of what a brand shows is text. The typeface is the voice a reader hears before he understands a word: one sentence set in two faces is two different promises. Two faces are enough: one to speak up, one to be read.

**Why now.** The voice is decided in words; the logo and the color are fixed. The type has to sound like the first and sit beside the other two.

**What we will leave with.** A type pair with its sizes and its license, and the headline: the **Type idea**.

From here the Art Director leads.

## Dig

1. What will people read from you most: a few large lines, or long text?
2. Should the letters feel written by a person, built by an engineer, or printed in a book?
3. Which languages must the type cover, now and in two years?

## Sharpness tests

- The headline face is recognizable in one word, without the logo.
- The text face reads easily in a paragraph at 16 pixels on a phone.
- Both faces have every letter of the market language.
- The license allows everything the brand will do with the type.
- A line set in this pair sounds like the Voice.

## Link

Next: the Motto. The brand has a shape, a color and letters; now it gets the few words it signs its copy with.

## Notes for the agency

- The Strategist gives the briefing and the benchmark. From the `Dig` on, the Art Director leads and signs once: `**Art Director:**`.
- The Dig has three questions. Question 3 starts from the market language in `pipeline.yaml`; ask only for what comes after it.
- The minting of this session is the choice of a type pair on a choice page, as the skill describes. When the pair is fixed, the Art Director proposes the `Type idea` in words, and the CEO accepts or edits it.
- A face has an open license (OFL, Apache or UFL), unless the `Constraints` of the Creative Brief record another license the CEO holds. Every face covers the market language; check before a pair is shown, not after.
- `Display` and `Text` are one line each: `Family - weights - role`. They may be the same family.
- `Sizes` is three lines, `Headline`, `Lead` and `Body`, each `Name: N px at 1440, N px at 390`, taken from the fixed variant.
- `License` is one line that names the license of each family.
- `Type idea` is at most 12 words.
- The headline slot is `Type idea`.
