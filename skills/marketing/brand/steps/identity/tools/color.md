# Color

Fixed text of session 2 of 4 of the identity. Show `Briefing` word for word; ask the `Dig` questions as written; show the `Sharpness tests` as a checklist; say the `Link` as written.

## Briefing

**Strategist:** Session 2 of 4: **Color**.

**Where it comes from.** Studio practice, no single author. One thought is borrowed from Josef Albers, "Interaction of Color", 1963: a color is almost never seen as it really is; it changes with the colors beside it.

**The idea.** A brand does not need many colors. It needs one it can own, and the discipline to keep the rest quiet. The palette is a background, a text color and one accent. The accent means something only because it is rare.

**Why now.** The logo holds in black and white. Color is what a person recognizes first, before the shape and long before the words.

**What we will leave with.** A palette of three to six colors for a light and a dark theme, with the contrast measured, and the headline: the **Color idea**.

From here the Art Director leads.

## Dig

1. Which color in your category is already taken, and by whom?
2. Is there a color you cannot stand, or one that means the wrong thing in your market?
3. Where will the color be seen most: on a screen, in print, on a physical thing?

## Sharpness tests

- One color carries the brand: with the logo covered, the accent alone still says who it is.
- No competitor from Positioning owns this accent.
- Body text reaches 4.5 to 1 on its background in both themes.
- "Accent is never for" forbids something a designer would be tempted to do.
- Nothing in the palette is in Excludes.

## Link

Next: Typography. The brand has a shape and a color; now it gets the letters that carry every word it will say.

## Notes for the agency

- The Strategist gives the briefing and the benchmark. From the `Dig` on, the Art Director leads and signs once: `**Art Director:**`.
- The Dig has three questions. For question 1, bring what you know: the alternatives of the Positioning statement in the Creative Brief and the category references on the board.
- The minting of this session is the choice of a palette on a choice page, as the skill describes. When the palette is fixed, the Art Director proposes the `Color idea` and the two accent lines in words, and the CEO accepts or edits them.
- `Palette` is 3 to 6 lines, one per color: `Name - #RRGGBB - role`. The roles are `background on light`, `text on light`, `background on dark`, `text on dark`, `accent` and `secondary`; a color may carry several, separated by commas. Each of the first five is carried by exactly one color; `secondary` by at most one.
- These are the brand's colors only. The colors an interface needs (error, success, grays) belong to the design system.
- `Contrast` is never written by hand: it is what `check_contract.py contrast` prints for the palette. A palette the command refuses is not shown.
- `Color idea` is at most 12 words.
- The headline slot is `Color idea`.
