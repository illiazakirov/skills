## Briefing

**Strategist:** Session 4 of 7: **Positioning**.

**Where it comes from.** The method: April Dunford, *Obviously Awesome*, 2019. The form of the final sentence is adapted from Geoffrey Moore, *Crossing the Chasm*, 1991.

**The idea.** Positioning is the context that lets a customer understand at once what you are and why it matters. Dunford's rule is the order: begin with what the customer would use instead of you, then what only you have, then what that is worth to them, then who cares most, and only then the category you belong in.

**Why now.** You have a customer and a job. This plate decides what you are compared with, and why you win that comparison.

**What we will leave with.** Five components and one **positioning statement**.

## Dig

Keep Dunford's order. Do not draft the statement before the five components are settled.

1. If you disappeared tomorrow, what would your best customer use instead? Include doing nothing and doing it by hand.
2. What do you have that none of those have?
3. For each: so what? What does it let the customer do, and what proves it?
4. Who cares about that far more than everyone else?
5. Next to what do you want to be placed, so that this value is obvious?

## Sharpness tests

- The alternatives are what customers would really use, not who you would like to be compared with.
- Every value line traces back to one attribute, and every attribute has proof.
- A customer who hears the category knows what to compare you with.
- Put a competitor's name into the statement and it becomes false.
