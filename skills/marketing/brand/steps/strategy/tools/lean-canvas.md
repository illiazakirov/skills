## Briefing

**Strategist:** Session 2 of 7: the **Lean Canvas**.

**Where it comes from.** Ash Maurya, 2010, in *Running Lean*; his rework of Alexander Osterwalder's Business Model Canvas for products that are still being figured out.

**The idea.** A whole business model on one page, in nine boxes. It starts from the customer's problem, not from your product, and it forces every box to be short enough that a weak one is visible at a glance.

**Why now.** You know why this exists. This plate checks that someone has a problem worth paying to solve.

**What we will leave with.** Nine boxes. The headline is the **Unique value proposition**: one sentence.

## Dig

Before asking, fill every box that already follows from The Brief and the Golden Circle, show it, and ask only about the rest.

1. Name your customer's three problems in the words they would use to complain.
2. What do they do about each one today?
3. Who feels this most sharply, enough to take a rough first version?
4. What do people pay you for today, and what should they pay for next?
5. Where do the money and the hours go?
6. Which single number tells you it is working?
7. What do you have that cannot be bought or copied within a year?

## Sharpness tests

- Each problem is a sentence a customer would say.
- Each solution line answers exactly one problem line.
- The unique value proposition says what the customer gets, not what the product is.
- The first key metric is a number you can read today.
- The unfair advantage cannot be bought.
