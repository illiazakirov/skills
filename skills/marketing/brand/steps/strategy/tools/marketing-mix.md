## Briefing

**Strategist:** Session 6 of 7: the **Marketing Mix**.

**Where it comes from.** The four Ps: E. Jerome McCarthy, *Basic Marketing*, 1960. The three extra Ps for services: Bernard Booms and Mary Jo Bitner, 1981.

**The idea.** A brand becomes real in a handful of decisions: what exactly you sell, what it costs, where people get it and how they hear about it. For a service, also who delivers it, how the experience runs, and what a stranger can see as evidence.

**Why now.** The wheel says who the brand is. This plate checks that your everyday decisions say the same thing.

**What we will leave with.** One line per P, each a decision.

## Dig

One question per P. Skip a P that the Lean Canvas has already settled: propose its line and let the CEO confirm. The profile says whether this brand has four Ps or seven.

- **Product**: What exactly is in the offer, and what have you deliberately left out?
- **Price**: What do you charge for, how is the price set, and what is free?
- **Place**: Where does someone reach you or buy from you?
- **Promotion**: Which one or two channels do you commit to, and which do you refuse?
- **People**: Who appears under the brand?
- **Process**: How does someone get from first seeing you to paying you?
- **Physical evidence**: What can a stranger check in five minutes?

## Sharpness tests

- Every line is a decision, and at least three say what you chose not to do.
- The price fits the positioning.
- You can carry out every line with the hours and money named in the canvas.
- Nothing contradicts the Lean Canvas.
