# ICP and Jobs-to-be-Done

Fixed text of session 3 of 7. Show `Briefing` word for word; ask the `Dig` questions as written; show the `Sharpness tests` as a checklist; say the `Link` as written.

## Briefing

**Strategist:** Session 3 of 7: **ICP and Jobs-to-be-Done**.

**Where it comes from.** Jobs-to-be-Done: Clayton Christensen (*The Innovator's Solution*, 2003; *Competing Against Luck*, 2016). The "When, I want to, so I can" job story came out of the product team at Intercom and was named by Alan Klement in 2013. The ideal customer profile comes from B2B sales practice.

**The idea.** Customers do not buy products; they hire them to get a job done. So we describe one customer by what can be observed, not by an invented persona, and then name the job they hire you for: its practical side, how they want to feel, and how they want to be seen.

**Why now.** The canvas named a segment. This plate turns it into one person you could go and find.

**What we will leave with.** One customer card and one **main job**, in a single sentence.

## Dig

1. Who has already come to you or paid you? Describe one real person.
2. What was happening in their work or life the week they started looking?
3. What had they tried before, and why was it not enough?
4. What do they want to be able to do afterwards?
5. How do they want to feel, and how do they want to look to others?
6. Where does their attention already go?
7. Who looks similar but is not your customer?

## Sharpness tests

- From the card alone you could find ten such people this week.
- The trigger is an event, not a mood.
- The main job does not mention your product.
- Nothing on the card is there only because it sounds like a persona (age, hobbies) without changing what you would do.

## Link

Next: Positioning. You know the customer and the job; next we decide what you are compared with, and why you win that comparison.
