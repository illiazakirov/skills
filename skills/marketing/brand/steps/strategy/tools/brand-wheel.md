# Brand Wheel

Fixed text of session 5 of 7. Show `Briefing` word for word; ask the `Dig` questions as written; show the `Sharpness tests` as a checklist; say the `Link` as written.

## Briefing

**Strategist:** Session 5 of 7: the **Brand Wheel**.

**Where it comes from.** The brand essence wheel, attributed to the advertising agency Bates Worldwide (formerly Ted Bates), mid-1990s.

**The idea.** Rings again, read from the outside in: what the brand is, what it gives people, what it stands for, how it behaves as a person, and at the center the essence, a few words that hold all of it.

**Why now.** The first four plates describe a business. This one describes a character. Voice, archetype and visual identity are all drawn from it.

**What we will leave with.** Five rings. The headline is the **Essence**: two to four words.

## Dig

Before asking, draft the whole wheel from the four earlier plates and show it. Ask only where you had to guess.

1. How do people who have worked with you describe you to others? Use their words.
2. If the brand walked into a room, how would it behave, and what would it never do?
3. What do you stand for that you would keep even if it cost you customers?
4. What does a customer feel in the first minute after working with you?
5. Which brand, in any field, should yours feel like, and which one never?

## Sharpness tests

- Each personality trait has an opposite that a sensible brand could choose.
- Each value has already cost you something.
- Every ring points to the essence.
- The essence is not a description of your category.
- Nothing contradicts the Golden Circle.
