Strategy 2 of 7

Lean Canvas

Problem

  • Computers are built for IT departments, not for people.
  • Getting online takes hours, cables and a manual.
  • Every PC is the same beige box.

Solution

  • Hardware and software designed together by one company.
  • iMac: plug in, switch on, be online in minutes.
  • Design as a reason to buy: one piece, color, translucency.

Unique value proposition

A computer that just works, and that you want on your desk.

Unfair advantage

The only PC maker that owns both the hardware and the operating system.

Customer segments

  • Households buying a first computer.
  • Designers and publishers.

Existing alternatives

A Windows PC from Compaq, Dell or Gateway.

Key metrics

  • Macs sold per quarter.
  • Share of buyers new to Mac.
  • Gross margin.

Channels

  • Apple's online store.
  • Apple sections inside large retailers.
  • Apple's own stores, from 2001.

Early adopter

Mac users who are about to give up on Apple.

Cost structure

  • Research and industrial design.
  • Contract manufacturing.
  • Advertising.

Revenue streams

  • Computers sold at a premium margin.
  • Software and accessories.

Strategy 3 of 7

ICP and Jobs-to-be-Done

Who

A household buying its first computer.

  1. Situation

    Friends and colleagues are getting email addresses; the family has no computer at home.

  2. Trigger

    A child's school, or a relative abroad, starts using email.

  3. Tried before

    Walked into a store, looked at Windows PCs, left confused by specifications and cables.

  4. Where to find

    Electronics retailers, television, general-interest magazines.

Main job

When everyone I know is getting online, I want to get there without learning how computers work, so I can take part without feeling stupid.

Functional

Have email and the web working the same day.

Emotional

Feel capable, not intimidated.

Social

Be seen as someone who keeps up, and has taste.

Other customers

  • Designers and publishers When my income depends on my tools, I want a machine built for creative work, so I can stay with the craft.
  • Teachers When I have thirty students and no technician, I want computers that simply run, so I can teach.

Strategy 6 of 7

Marketing Mix

  1. Product

    Four computers only: consumer and pro, desktop and portable, each designed together with its software.

  2. Price

    Premium and never discounted; the iMac at $1,299 is the way in.

  3. Place

    Apple's online store, Apple sections inside large retailers, and from 2001 its own stores.

  4. Promotion

    The Think Different campaign on television, print and billboards; every product launch staged as an event.

  5. People

    Steve Jobs as the single public voice; trained staff in Apple's own stores.

  6. Process

    Buy, unbox, plug in, online: minutes, with no technician.

  7. Physical evidence

    The object itself, its packaging, and the stores.

Messaging

Main promise

A computer that gets out of the way of your ideas.

Proofs

  • Online in minutesplug in the power and the phone line; there is no third step.
  • One company makes all of itApple designs the computer and its operating system together.

Voice

Voice

We sound like a friend who knows computers, never like a manual.

Tone

FormalCasual

One person talking to another across a table.

SeriousFunny

Wit in the headline, straight in the facts.

RespectfulIrreverent

We write like this

  • A headline may play with words.
  • Speak to "you".

We never write

  • Words only engineers use.
  • A sentence that needs a second reading.

Identity 1 of 4

Logo

Logo idea

The same apple, without the stripes: the shape alone is enough.

Kind

Symbol and wordmark

Construction

An apple silhouette with a bite out of the right side and a detached leaf, in one solid color.

Minimum size

  • Full logo 24 px high
  • Mark 16 px high

Clear space

A quarter of the apple's height on every side.

Never

  • More than one color at a time
  • Redrawn, stretched or tilted
  • On a background that swallows the silhouette

Design system

Interface

Interface idea

Controls you want to touch: liquid, lit from above, never flat.

Shape

Controls 22 px, cards 14 px, badges fully round.

Depth

Raised soft shadows under controls and cards, hairline borders on fields.

Density

8 px unit, 44 px controls

Motion

180 ms, eased out; a pressed button sinks one pixel.

Never

  • A square button.
  • A shadow under text.
  • Two raised controls side by side in the accent.

How to use this

25

Read first. This book, from the start. The strategy says what the brand promises and to whom; the platform says how it sounds; the identity and the design system say how it looks.

Fixed. The logo files, the palette, the two families and their sizes, the motto, and every value in tokens.json. Use them as they are. Never redraw the logo, add a color or set a size by hand.

Free. The composition of a page, the images, the length of a text, the order of sections, within the rules of this book.

Check before you ship.

  • Text reaches 4.5 to 1 on its background, and the borders of controls 3 to 1, in both themes.
  • No line breaks a rule of We never write.
  • The accent appears only where Accent is for allows it.
  • The logo is never smaller than its Minimum size and keeps its Clear space.

When something is missing. Do not improvise. Name what is missing and go back to the step that owns it: the strategy, the platform, the identity or the design system.
